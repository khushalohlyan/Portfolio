01 Overview

One database, two sides of the same mission

GoDiverse gives large corporates and tier 1 suppliers access to a comprehensive portal of diverse suppliers in the UK. The Unified Database is the product's core: one place to search thousands of qualified diverse businesses and turn them into evidence based shortlists.

My role

Product designer, end to end: research synthesis, information architecture, interaction design, visual UI, design system, and engineering handoff.

Timeline

8 weeks from kickoff to dev handoff, followed by 4 weeks of iteration support after launch.

Platform

Responsive web application, designed desktop first because buyers work from primary screens.

Team

1 product manager, 4 engineers, and me as the only designer.

Tools

Figma and FigJam for design and flows, Maze for moderated testing, Notion for research synthesis.

Deliverables

Research synthesis, a field level information architecture covering both buyer and supplier sides, 40+ designed screens, a component based design system, and a clickable prototype.

02 The Challenge

Teams believed in the mission. The tooling kept letting them down.

Supplier diversity teams wanted to buy from diverse businesses. But discovery ran on static lists, spreadsheet exports, and email threads, so shortlists took days and verification happened by hand.

Pain 01

Fragmented data

Supplier records lived in spreadsheets, PDF directories, and inboxes. There was no single source of truth and no consistency between teams.

Pain 02

Slow discovery

With no keyword search, buyers scrolled endlessly or asked around the office. Assembling a credible shortlist could take days.

Pain 03

A trust gap

Buyers could not tell certified diverse suppliers from self identified ones, so every shortlist needed slow manual verification.

Pain 04

Single player in a team sport

Procurement decisions involve buyers, managers, and compliance. Favorites were copy pasted over email, causing version chaos.

Pain 05

Manual reporting

Diversity spend reports for leadership were assembled by hand in slide decks, weeks after the fact.

Pain 06

Suppliers stayed invisible

Diverse businesses wanted to be found but generic directories buried them, and long forms pushed them to abandon their profiles.

The stakes: when buyers cannot find or trust diverse suppliers quickly, they default to familiar incumbents. Supplier diversity goals quietly slip, and the mission loses by default.

06 Product Walkthrough

Eight moments that carry the experience

Each screen below serves one of the six steps in the money flow. No dead ends, no decoration without a job.

Role selection and authenticationScreens 01 to 02

01 Onboarding

One question before anything else

The welcome screen asks a single question: which option describes you? One tap decides the entire workspace. Buyers are handed to a guided get in touch flow to request a demo, matching how enterprise procurement actually buys. Suppliers dive straight into self-serve registration with a business email and OTP verification, then personal details, company selection, and a full diversity declaration. The companion auth screen keeps the same calm illustration language and pairs a one field sign in with a lightweight sign up, so returning users never re register and new ones never hit a wall of forms.

Design decisionWe tested a three step wizard first. Users hesitated at step two every single time, and a single binary choice removed the hesitation entirely. Routing buyers to a demo request rather than self-serve signup also matched the enterprise sales motion we heard about in stakeholder interviews.
Unified Search with one tap keyword chipsScreen 03

02 Unified Search

Search that speaks human

Discovery begins with one question: what are you looking for? A buyer types Manchester and the database responds instantly. One tap keyword chips cover the most common queries, from London to Women Owned Business. Advanced structure waits politely behind a single Filters button instead of crowding the first screen.

Design decisionBuyers think in natural language, like women owned firms near Manchester, not in field by field queries. A single search field plus suggestion chips matched the mental model we heard in every interview.
Results table with active filters as removable chipsScreen 04

03 Results

Results built for the scan

The results table carries the product's core promise in one row: company, industry, diversity type, business size, location, and employee count. Active filters live as removable chips above the table, so the current query is always legible and always reversible. A compare toggle marks rows for side by side evaluation.

IterationAn early card grid tested poorly with buyers who live in spreadsheets. The dense, sortable table cut time to shortlist by more than half in the next round of testing.
Supplier detail opened in place, results preservedScreen 05

04 Supplier detail

Depth without losing context

Clicking a row opens a detail panel for suppliers like Eco Tech Innovation Inc.: a plain language overview, founding year, industry classification, headquarters, the leadership team, and direct contact details, without leaving the results behind. Buyers confirm fit in seconds and return exactly where they left off.

Design decisionA separate profile page meant a full navigation away and a broken back button state. The overlay kept results alive underneath, and buyers scanned three suppliers in the time one page load used to take.
My saved list workspace with persisted filtersScreen 06

05 My Saved List

Shortlists that stick

Buyers save suppliers into My Saved List, a dedicated workspace where active filters persist as chips. A shortlist becomes a durable artifact: revisit it next week, refine it with new filters, and hand it to the team ready to argue with. Pagination keeps large sets honest, 10 of 40 entries, with no infinite scroll ambush.

Design decisionShortlists used to be copy pasted into email. Giving them a permanent home with visible filters ended the version chaos we heard about in the diary study.
Supplier side profile editor with strength meterScreen 07

06 Supplier side

Profiles worth trusting

Suppliers build the other half of the database. The company profile editor mirrors exactly what buyers see: overview, contact details, leadership, and firm facts like industry, founding year, and size. A completion meter shows progress at a glance and nudges richer data, because every completed field is a better answer to someone else's search.

Design decisionA visible percentage meter with clear next fields beat badge gamification in testing. Suppliers wanted to know what was missing, not how they ranked.
Team roster with inline roles and email verified invitesScreen 08

07 Team management

Procurement is a team sport

Enterprise buyers invite colleagues with a shareable link, verify them over email, and manage the roster from one table: name, job title, and role at a glance, with a one tap make admin and an inline remove. Ownership stays visible so accountability is never a mystery, and permissions never require a separate settings maze.

Design decisionOne invite link beat individual email invites for enterprise onboarding: an admin drops the link in an internal channel, email verification keeps the roster clean, and the workspace fills itself.
Upload flow and the enrichment dashboardScreens 09 to 10

08 Data Enrichment

From CSV to boardroom story

Teams upload supplier or spend CSVs, select the files to process, and submit. GoDiverse enriches each record with verified diversity data. The dashboard then answers leadership's questions in order: total diverse suppliers, spend amounts, the split between certified and self identified, ownership mix by group in one donut, and regional concentration on a live map backed by a top regions table. In the pilot workspace, 750 records flowed through in a single quarter.

Design decisionThe four KPI tiles sit above the fold, then the donut, map, and table answer follow up questions in the order stakeholders actually ask them: who, where, and how much. Past enrichment files stay in the rail so quarter over quarter comparison is one click away.

08 Reflection

What I am taking to the next project

Learning 01

Design for the spreadsheet brain

In B2B tools, density done well beats whitespace done for its own sake. Ship tables that respect the people who live in columns and rows.

Learning 02

Trust is a UI element

A certification chip did more for buyer confidence than any copy we wrote. Show the evidence exactly where the decision happens.

Learning 03

One fork, then get out of the way

The single role question outperformed every onboarding pattern we tried because it asks once and never nags again.

Learning 04

Data needs a storyteller

The enrichment dashboard landed because it answered questions in the order stakeholders ask them, not the order the database stores them.

If I had the next sprint, I would push for

  • Saved search alerts that notify buyers when new matching suppliers join the database
  • In app messaging connecting buyers and suppliers at the exact moment of intent
  • One click board reports in corporate branding, generated straight from the dashboard
