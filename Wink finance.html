01 Overview

A DeFi earning platform with one job: make yield feel safe.

WINK Finance is a decentralized earning platform built on Polygon. Users deposit WINK or its stablecoin USDW and earn sustainable on-chain yield (Earn USDW: APY 8–20% · Earn WINK: APR 29.55%), convert tokens, and manage their entire portfolio from one dashboard.

I owned the product surface end-to-end: the marketing landing page, wallet onboarding, the portfolio dashboard, earning & deposit flows, the token conversion interface, account management, and the design system that binds it all together.

The core design question: how do you communicate trust in an industry where a single misunderstood button can cost someone their savings?
🪙

The product in one flow

Connect a wallet → view balances (WINK · USDW · USDT) → deposit into an earning program → convert or send tokens → track earnings year-to-date.

📈

Why it matters

Yield platforms historically lose the majority of first-time users at wallet connection. Every screen I designed had to lower that wall.

02 The Problem

DeFi rewards your money but punishes your attention.

Yield platforms are engineered by protocol engineers, for protocol engineers. The result: interfaces that assume you already know what a wallet is, why you must “approve” before you deposit, and what APY even means.

🚧

The onboarding wall

Wallet connection is the single biggest drop-off in Web3. Users hit a modal full of unfamiliar names (MetaMask? Rainbow? WalletConnect?) with no explanation of what any of it means, and leave.

🔤

Jargon as a barrier

APY vs APR, TVL, gas, allowances, approvals. Each unexplained term compounds anxiety. Users don't just want bigger numbers. They want to know what happens to their funds.

😶

Silent transactions

Multi-step flows (Approve → Deposit) go quiet while the browser extension takes over. With no feedback, users assume it's broken and abort mid-transaction.

🎯 How might we…

help a crypto-curious user go from landing page to earning their first yield in under two minutes, confidently and without opening a single support doc?

03 Research

Studying how people fail at earning money.

I ran a heuristic evaluation of six leading platforms (Aave, Compound, Beefy.Finance, Curve, Lido, QuickSwap) and combined it with moderated walkthroughs where participants narrated their hesitation out loud.

Key findings

1
Wallet connection kills momentum

Most first-time participants stalled at the wallet modal, not because it was hard, but because nothing explained why they needed it or which option to pick.

2
APY vs APR was invisible friction

Nearly every participant compared the two numbers as equals. Nobody knew one compounds and the other doesn't, or that one rate is stable and the other variable.

3
“Approve” read as “give away my money”

The two-step Approve → Deposit pattern was consistently misread as being charged twice, or as granting the site access to move funds freely.

4
TVL and social proof drive trust

Participants judged legitimacy from visible numbers (total value locked, recognizable wallet brands, and a clean, calm interface) before reading a single word of copy.

5
Empty states felt like dead ends

A dashboard full of $0.00 balances read as “broken,” not “new.” Users had no idea what to do next once connected.

🧪 Methods used

Heuristic evaluation (6 platforms) · 9 moderated walkthroughs (5 DeFi-natives, 4 newcomers) · unmoderated task tests on prototypes · competitor pattern library · funnel analysis of abandoned onboarding steps.

👥 Two personas, one interface

“Yield Farmer Yara” wants rates, routes and speed. Hates friction, tolerates jargon.

“Curious Sam” has some MATIC, has MetaMask installed, has never deposited anywhere. Needs reassurance at every step. Sam is the design constraint.

04 Design Principles

Four rules that shaped every screen.

01

Trust before yield

Security signals, TVL, and honest permission language come before rates and CTAs. If a user hesitates, we've already lost.

02

Plain language, always

“What's a wallet?” is answered inline, not behind a docs link. Every term a first-timer meets gets a one-line human explanation.

03

Progressive disclosure

Rates and balances up front; routes, contracts, and advanced detail on demand. Complexity is earned, never forced.

04

Never a dead end

Every state, especially empty ones, ends in exactly one clear next action. A $0 dashboard is a starting line, not a wall.

06 Key Screens

Annotated walkthrough of the core experience.

Each screen below is a real artifact of the shipped product, with the decisions behind it. Click any image to enlarge.

Screen 01 / 08

Landing: the earn hub

The landing page doubles as the product's front door and its pitch. Two earning programs sit side by side, with total value locked acting as an immediate trust signal.

  • Rates before login. APY/APR and TVL ($456,970 · $18.4M) are visible without connecting, with no info-walls for curious visitors.
  • Stable vs variable is labeled, not implied. USDW reads as “fixed price / fixed rate”; WINK as “variable price / variable rate.”
  • One repeated CTA. “Connect your wallet and start earning!” appears at the top and again at the bottom of the fold. Same action, everywhere.
  • Nav trimmed to intent: Home · Earn · Docs + Dashboard · Connect.
Screen 02 / 08

Dashboard empty state

Before a wallet is connected, the dashboard resists the temptation to fake content. Instead, it states the situation and offers exactly one way out.

  • One message, one button. “Connect your wallet to see your dashboard” + a single blue CTA. Zero competing links.
  • Functional furniture stays. Nav and controls remain visible so the page feels alive, not locked.
  • The empty state is a guide, not a wall — principle 04 in practice.
Screen 03 / 08

Connect-a-wallet modal

The highest-stakes screen in the product. The two-column layout serves two audiences at once: pick fast, or learn first.

  • Detected wallets rank first. Installed options (MetaMask, tagged “Recent”) sit above “Popular” ones (Rainbow, Coinbase Wallet, WalletConnect): fewer decisions, fewer mistakes.
  • Inline education. A “What's a wallet?” panel explains digital assets and wallet-as-login in plain words, with “Get a wallet” / “Learn more” paths.
  • Never trap the user. Someone without a wallet still has a next step; nothing dead-ends.
Screen 04 / 08

The extension handshake

The moment control leaves the page is the moment users panic. I designed an explicit intermediate state so the handoff never feels like silence.

  • Explicit status copy. “Opening MetaMask… Confirm connection in the extension” says exactly what's happening and where to look next.
  • Honest permissions. The MetaMask sheet shows “See addresses,” “Send requests,” and crucially “Cannot move funds,” building trust through radical clarity about limits.
  • Context persists. The modal stays open behind the extension so returning users re-orient instantly.
Screen 05 / 08

Portfolio dashboard

Once connected, the dashboard answers three questions in one glance: what do I have, what is it doing, and what can I do with it right now?

  • Headline numbers: total balance and current-year P/L sit above the fold, forming the emotional core of a finance product.
  • Actions live on the asset. Each token row (WINK · USDW · USDT) carries its own Convert / Earn / Send buttons, so there's no hunting through menus.
  • Earning programs persist (Earn USDW 8–20% APY · Earn WINK 29.55% APR) with “start earning” adjacent to the balances that fund them.
  • Tabular figures keep dollar columns optically aligned as numbers change.
Screen 06 / 08

Earn hub with deposits

For returning users, the earn hub pairs each program's headline stats with the wallet panel that funds it, so decisions happen where the numbers are.

  • Program cards lead with TVL and rate ($456,970 / $18.4M locked), then a single “Start Earning” action.
  • Wallet panel below: per-token Deposit buttons plus a global “Convert Tokens” control, putting funding and earning in one viewport.
  • “Buy WINK” and “Dashboard” anchored top-right so the two most common intents are always one click away.
Screen 07 / 08

Convert tokens

Swapping is where DeFi UX most often breaks, so the Actions panel enforces the correct sequence instead of hoping users know it.

  • Two-step, visibly sequenced: ① “Approve WINK” → ② “Convert WINK to USDT.” The second stays disabled until the first completes, so failed transactions are designed out rather than warned about.
  • Route transparency: the trade overview shows the full route and resulting value before anything is signed.
  • From/To as first-class controls with token previews, so the trade is legible before a single number is entered.
Screen 08 / 08

Account panel

In Web3, your wallet is your account. The account modal treats it accordingly: identity, balance, and safe exit. Nothing else.

  • Identity as address: the green avatar + truncated address (0x27…74EC) with network balance (0 POL): recognizable, copyable, never overwhelming.
  • Safe action separated from dangerous: “Copy Address” (reversible) and “Disconnect” (session-ending) are visually distinct so the destructive-ish action can't be hit by reflex.
  • Persistent context. The dashboard stays visible behind the modal, so the user never loses their place.

📐 Grid & spacing

4-pt spacing scale; 16–24px radii on cards and modals; consistent 24px section rhythm so every page breathes the same way.

🧩 Components

Buttons (primary / ghost / disabled / destructive-adjacent), modal shell, token rows, stat blocks, rate badges, and toasts, with 40+ variants documented across states.

♿ Accessibility

AA contrast on dark surfaces, visible focus rings, icon+label pairs (never icon-only for money actions), and motion reduced to essential feedback.

08 Challenges & Solutions

Where the hard design work happened.

Challenge

Users read “Approve” as “give away my funds”

The ERC-20 allowance step was the most feared interaction in testing; participants hesitated longest here and some aborted entirely.

Solution

Permission honesty + enforced sequence

Language mirrors the wallet's own (“Cannot move funds”), and the Actions panel disables “Convert” until “Approve” completes, making the safe order the only order.

Challenge

APY vs APR compared as equals

Users saw “8–20%” next to “29.55%” and naturally chased the bigger number without understanding stable vs variable risk.

Solution

Rate badges that carry the risk

Color-coded chips (green = stable APY · violet = variable APR) with plain-language tooltips, so the label itself teaches the trade-off.

Challenge

Silent handoffs to the wallet extension

When MetaMask takes over, the page goes quiet; users assume the site froze and close it mid-connection.

Solution

Explicit intermediate states

A persistent “Opening MetaMask… Confirm connection in the extension” state names what happened and where to look next, with the modal waiting on return.

Challenge

$0 dashboards felt broken

Newly connected users saw all-zero balances with no path forward, a dead end at the exact moment of highest intent.

Solution

Every empty state is a starting line

Zero-balance tokens carry inline Deposit / Convert actions, and program cards with “Start Earning” sit in the same viewport, so the next step is always visible.

10 Reflections

What this project taught me.

01

Web3 UX is trust design

Every hesitation in DeFi is really the same question: “Is my money safe?” The answers (honest permission language, visible TVL, sequenced actions, explicit states) are design decisions, not copywriting garnish.

02

Empty states are teaching moments

A screen full of zeros is the product's one guaranteed conversation with every new user. Spending design effort there beats polishing screens power users visit twice.

03

You design for two users: the human and the wallet

Half the experience happens inside an extension I don't control. Great Web3 design choreographs the handoff, naming what's happening and leaving a trail of breadcrumbs back.

04

Constraints make the interface honest

Disabling “Convert” until “Approve” finishes felt restrictive at first, but testing showed it converted confusion into confidence. Guardrails communicate care.
